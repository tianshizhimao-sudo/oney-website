## The Numbers That Matter

When the RBA raises rates, lenders adjust their serviceability assessments. Here's the real impact on your borrowing power:

**For a single income:**
- $80K salary: Borrowing capacity down ~$28K
- $100K salary: Borrowing capacity down ~$35K
- $120K salary: Borrowing capacity down ~$42K

**For dual income ($100K combined):**
- Borrowing capacity down ~$35K
- On a $600K loan: Monthly repayments up $174
- Over 30 years: Additional ~$62,640 in interest

**What this means in practice:**
A property you could afford in January 2026 might now be out of reach. Or you'll need to increase your deposit to compensate for the reduced borrowing capacity.

## Why This Happened

The RBA raised rates to control inflation. Here's the timeline:

**February 2026:** Cash rate increased from 3.85% to 3.95%  
**April 2026:** Cash rate increased from 3.95% to 4.10%

Banks pass on these increases to variable mortgage rates, which affects:
1. **Existing borrowers:** Higher monthly repayments
2. **New borrowers:** Reduced borrowing capacity (banks test at a higher rate)

## What You Can Do

### 1. Know Your Real Numbers

Don't guess your borrowing capacity based on online calculators. Get a comprehensive assessment that factors in:
- Your actual income (including bonuses, overtime, rental income)
- Your debts (credit cards, car loans, HECS)
- Your expenses (living costs, subscriptions, dependents)
- Current bank serviceability buffers

### 2. Optimize Your Financial Position

Before applying, strengthen your application:

**Reduce debts:**
- Pay down credit cards (even if you pay them off monthly, the limit affects you)
- Clear small loans where possible
- Reduce credit card limits

**Improve income:**
- Document all income sources (don't miss rental, side income)
- Get bonuses in writing from employer
- Show consistent income over 3-6 months

**Clean up expenses:**
- Cancel unused subscriptions
- Reduce discretionary spending in the 3 months before applying
- Avoid buy-now-pay-later (Afterpay, Zip)

### 3. Adjust Your Timeline or Budget

With reduced capacity, you have three options:

**Option A: Wait and save more**
- Increase deposit to compensate for lower borrowing
- Use the time to optimize your financial position
- Monitor rates (they might stabilize or drop)

**Option B: Adjust your budget**
- Target properties within new capacity range
- Consider different suburbs or property types
- Look at smaller apartments or townhouses

**Option C: Improve serviceability**
- Increase income (second job, side hustle, pay rise)
- Reduce fixed expenses
- Pay off debts aggressively

## What's Next for Rates?

**RBA Signals:**
The RBA has indicated rates may stabilize at this level if inflation moderates. No immediate further rises are expected, but the cash rate is unlikely to drop significantly in 2026.

**What This Means:**
- Current rates may be the "new normal" for 2026
- Don't wait for rates to drop before acting
- Focus on what you can control (your financial position)

**Bank Behavior:**
Not all banks pass on rate changes equally. Some offer:
- Lower variable rates for new customers
- Cashback offers to offset higher rates
- Fixed rate options to lock in certainty

## The First Home Buyer Advantage

Despite rate rises, first home buyers still have advantages:

**First Home Guarantee:**
- Buy with 5% deposit (government guarantees remaining 15%)
- Avoid LMI (Lenders Mortgage Insurance)
- Available for properties up to regional price caps

**First Home Owner Grant:**
- $10K-$30K depending on state (for new builds)
- Can boost your deposit

**Regional Price Caps:**
- FHBG caps vary by location
- Consider regional areas with higher caps
