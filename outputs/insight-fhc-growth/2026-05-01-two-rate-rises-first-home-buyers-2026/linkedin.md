# Two Rate Rises in 2026: The Real Impact on First Home Buyer Capacity

The RBA has raised rates twice this year, bringing the cash rate to 4.10%. While headlines focus on the percentage points, the real story is in the numbers that matter to aspiring homeowners.

**The Data:**

For a household earning $100K with a 20% deposit:
- Borrowing capacity has decreased by approximately $35K
- Monthly repayments on a $600K loan have increased by $174
- This represents a 5-8% reduction in purchasing power since January 2026

**What This Means:**

Many first home buyers who were "ready" at the start of 2026 now face strategic choices:

1. **Adjust budget and target range** — Consider different suburbs, property types, or sizes within new capacity
2. **Increase deposit to compensate** — Save additional funds to offset reduced borrowing power
3. **Optimize financial position** — Reduce debts, improve income documentation, clean up expenses

**Market Context:**

The RBA has signaled rates may stabilize at this level if inflation moderates. This suggests:
- Current rates could be the "new normal" for 2026
- Waiting for significant rate drops may delay plans unnecessarily
- Focus should shift to controllable factors (financial optimization)

**Strategic Implications:**

First home buyers still have advantages despite rate increases:
- First Home Guarantee (5% deposit, no LMI)
- First Home Owner Grant ($10K-$30K depending on state)
- Regional price caps vary by location

The key is understanding your specific position within this environment.

**The Professional Approach:**

Before making strategic decisions, get clear data on your specific situation. Understanding your exact borrowing capacity, debt-to-income ratio, and optimization opportunities is fundamental to making informed choices in this market.
